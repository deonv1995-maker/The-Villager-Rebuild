# Game Vision

## High concept

The Villager is a mobile-first 3D survival and settlement-building game set on a fantasy island. The player always controls one main Ranger who begins alone after a shipwreck and gradually becomes the founder and manager of a living settlement.

The game should remain approachable rather than becoming a deep survival simulator or a complex city-management spreadsheet. The core satisfaction comes from personally learning and performing survival tasks first, then gradually delegating repetitive work to recruited villagers while retaining direct control of the Ranger.

## Player fantasy

The player progresses through three identities without changing characters:

1. Survivor — gather, hunt, cook, build a fire and stay alive.
2. Builder — create a custom home base/town centre and establish storage.
3. Founder — recruit villagers, place homes, assign jobs, build production and grow a settlement.

Automation should create freedom rather than replace gameplay. Even late in the game, the player may still hunt, chop trees, gather resources or custom-build structures whenever desired.

## Design pillars

### Simple survival

The main survival stats are health, hunger and stamina. They should matter enough to create decisions but should not dominate play with excessive micromanagement.

### Learn by doing

The opening tutorial teaches the permanent game rules through real actions rather than menus. Each resource or important item can show a one-time discovery card explaining what it is used for.

### Physical world logic

Resources exist in the world and move through understandable loops. Trees become logs. Hunters produce meat. Villagers gather resources and deliver them to settlement storage rather than feeding an invisible economy wherever practical.

### Player-designed settlement

The Ranger custom-builds important structures, especially the first home base/town centre. Villager homes and some NPC-oriented buildings use predefined blueprints to keep automation manageable and mobile-friendly.

### Living villagers without excessive simulation

Villagers should feel alive through visible work, travel, home ownership, day/night routines, roaming and simple personal identity. The game does not require deep personality statistics or complicated needs simulation.

### Exploration through concealment

The island should feel extensive without being computationally expensive. Tall forest, dense canopy, curved paths, clearings, elevation, cliffs and limited sightlines conceal the horizon and make a compact seamless island feel larger.

### Moderate danger

The world contains some hostile beings, including skeleton-like enemies that attack when they see the player. Combat should add tension but should not become the primary game genre or be excessively difficult.

## Population scale

The island should contain no more than approximately 30 recruitable villagers. A smaller named population allows each villager to have a persistent home, job and identity while remaining technically appropriate for mobile hardware.

## Long-term goal

The player survives, establishes a settlement, recruits the island's survivors, develops gathering and food production, introduces specialized production, expands settlement infrastructure and eventually grows a small camp into a fantasy island city.

The city stage should represent maturity and self-sufficiency rather than simply requiring a very large population.
