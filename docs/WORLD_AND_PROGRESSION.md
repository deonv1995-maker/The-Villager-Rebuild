# World and Progression

## Island design goal

Exploration should feel extensive without requiring an expensive physically enormous world.

The dominant technique is limited sightlines. The player should rarely be able to see more than one major destination at a time.

## Forest structure

The island should be dominated by tall forest trees and a dense canopy that hides the horizon. Forest corridors, bends, rocks, ridges and undergrowth should prevent long uninterrupted views.

Open spaces should be used selectively as contrast and reward: clearings, meadows, survivor camps, hunting areas, ponds, rocky shelves and other landmarks.

Paths should generally curve and disappear behind vegetation or terrain rather than forming long straight roads.

The sea should be most visible near beaches, cliffs and chosen viewpoints rather than from most of the island interior.

## Performance strategy

The visual design should support mobile hardware through reusable environment sets, instancing, level-of-detail strategies, culling and carefully controlled active simulation.

Dense canopy is useful both aesthetically and technically because it conceals repetition and limits the amount of distant geometry that needs to be convincing at once.

## Suggested regional variety

The island does not need many full biomes. A small number of visually distinct regions is preferable to excessive asset variety.

Likely regions include:

- shipwreck/coastal edge;
- dominant deep forest;
- meadow or open valley areas;
- rocky highlands/cliffs;
- a darker or more dangerous forest region.

Exact regional design remains open until the environment asset packs are audited.

## Settlement location

The first settlement should be established in or near a broad sheltered clearing within reach of the coast but not exposed as a completely open beach settlement.

The surrounding forest should make the player feel as though civilization is gradually being carved out of wilderness.

## Settlement stages

### Camp

A Camp has the Town Centre, initial storage and houses but no meaningful production economy. Survival depends primarily on gathering and hunting.

### Village

A Village has at least three functional production buildings. Farming, cooking or other production starts reducing dependence on direct gathering and hunting.

### Town

A Town is broadly self-supporting. Several villagers hold specialized jobs, food production is reliable, construction is supplied by an organized resource flow and multiple workplaces contribute to the settlement economy.

The exact Town unlock requirements remain to be defined after the production system is designed.

### City

A City represents a mature fantasy settlement with distinct functional areas, specialized production, reliable food, larger civic capability and enough automation that the player is primarily expanding, exploring and managing rather than maintaining daily survival.

City status should not depend only on an arbitrary population number. The exact requirements remain intentionally undecided.

## Exploration and recruitment

Up to approximately 30 survivors are scattered around the island at small personal camps. Camps should be spaced so finding another survivor feels meaningful rather than routine.

Subtle environmental signals such as campfire glow, smoke, paths or visible belongings may help guide discovery without turning every survivor into a large map-marker objective.
