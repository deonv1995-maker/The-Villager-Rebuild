# Pickaxe Terrain Sculpting Architecture

## Decision

The Pickaxe is the single player-facing terrain tool.

While the Pickaxe is equipped, a compact top-right terrain dock exposes five modes in this order:

1. **Raise ground**
2. **Lower ground**
3. **Dig**
4. **Smoothen**
5. **Level**

The modes share one tool and one durability lifecycle, but they deliberately use two different terrain representations.

- **Dig** remains true 3D excavation owned by `UndergroundTunnelingSystem`. It can create tunnels, caves, underground rooms, floors, walls and ceilings.
- **Raise / Lower / Smoothen / Level** edit the 2D surface-height authority owned by `TerrainSculptingSystem`. They do not subtract arbitrary underground volume.

This separation keeps surface terraforming simple and mobile-friendly without weakening the existing volumetric tunneling architecture.

## Surface-height authority

`ExpandedIslandTerrainSystem` now exposes two height concepts:

- `naturalHeightAt(x, z)` — deterministic unmodified geology generated by the world seed/procedural terrain;
- `heightAt(x, z)` — the current player-editable surface after `TerrainSculptingSystem` applies persisted edits.

Existing world consumers continue to call `heightAt()`, so collision, slope queries, construction adaptation and terrain rendering see the edited surface without introducing a competing terrain system.

`TerrainSculptingSystem` installs one height modifier and stores compact brush operations in order. Edits are bucketed spatially so a height sample evaluates only operations that can influence that X/Z point.

Current first-pass brush tuning:

- radius: 2.4 m;
- Raise: +0.38 m at brush centre per use;
- Lower: -0.38 m at brush centre per use;
- Smoothen: blends toward a local surrounding-height average;
- Level: blends toward the height under the selected white-dot point;
- maximum accumulated surface offset: ±8 m from natural terrain.

Each operation uses a smooth radial falloff instead of a hard cylinder edge.

## Targeting and mobile controls

The existing first-person white-dot aim remains the terrain targeting authority.

When a surface mode is selected:

- normal Pickaxe rock mining and generic Dig targeting are suppressed;
- the selected terrain operation owns the unified mobile Action button;
- a circular brush preview is shown on the valid target surface;
- the normal Pickaxe swing presentation and durability consumption still apply.

When **Dig** is selected, the terrain controller releases Action ownership completely. Existing overworld rock mining and `UndergroundTunnelingSystem` targeting then behave exactly as before.

The terrain menu reuses the established compact top-right hammer/shovel presentation contract instead of adding another independent HUD layout.

## Terrain rendering, construction and vegetation

A surface edit rebuilds only terrain chunks intersecting the brush. Ordinary world chunks keep the established coarse mobile lattice; chunks containing surface edits move to a scoped medium-detail lattice so the 2.4 m brush is represented by visible geometry rather than only by collision math. Tunnel-opening chunks retain their higher-detail lattice because they also have to cut a real 3D hole through the heightfield.

`ConstructionTerrainAdaptationSystem` already listens for terrain geometry replacement. It refreshes its tracked natural vertex data, reapplies active floor cuts, and now advances the shared terrain revision when the underlying terrain geometry changes.

That revision is already consumed by grass, ferns, meadow ground-cover and jungle-floor presentation systems. They therefore reproject their Y positions onto the edited surface instead of remaining suspended at their pre-edit heights.

This keeps one terrain/collision source of truth and avoids a Pickaxe-specific vegetation system.

Large authored/scattered props such as trees and rocks remain owned by their existing world systems. They are not moved or deleted by this milestone.

## Tunneling interaction

The visible density ceiling continues to use the current edited `heightAt()`, so a player can reshape the surface and then tunnel naturally through that reshaped ground.

The protected tunneling-depth boundary and deterministic underground-pocket placement use `naturalHeightAt()` instead. This is intentional:

- lowering the surface after digging must not invalidate an existing saved tunnel;
- raising the surface must not silently increase the geological mining budget;
- deterministic pockets must not move because the player terraformed above them.

After a surface edit, active tunneling chunks intersecting that brush are rebuilt and surface openings are recalculated against the edited surface.

## Water ownership

The former base ocean plane and animated ocean-shimmer plane covered the entire map at sea level. A tunnel opening on inland solid ground could therefore expose those hidden planes.

Both ocean layers now share one **natural-water mask** generated from `naturalHeightAt()`.

A water triangle is emitted only where its sampled natural terrain vertices are submerged. Inland solid ground therefore has no hidden sea-level water geometry underneath it, so mining through solid land cannot reveal the ocean.

Player lowering of inland terrain below sea level does not create groundwater or a new lake in this milestone. Dynamic hydrology remains a separate future system.

## Persistence

Surface edits are stored in the existing save record under `state.terrainSculpting`.

Restore order is:

1. world time;
2. terrain sculpting;
3. underground tunneling;
4. construction and the remaining gameplay state.

Restoring surface edits before tunneling ensures the density mesh and dynamic surface openings are rebuilt against the same edited ground the player last saw.

The terrain-sculpting payload has its own state kind/schema and serializes only compact brush operations.

## Regression contract

`scripts/verify-pickaxe-terrain-sculpting.mjs` protects:

- the five requested modes and their order;
- land-aware masking for both base ocean and shimmer;
- no ocean geometry covering representative solid inland ground;
- first-person surface target acquisition;
- Raise and Lower changing the shared height authority;
- Level reducing local height differences;
- Smoothen remaining a surface operation;
- Dig staying outside the 2D surface editor;
- affected-chunk terrain rebuilds;
- terrain revision publication for vegetation reprojection;
- compact surface-edit persistence;
- surface restore before tunneling restore;
- continued 3D density-field mining after surface edits;
- active tunneling refresh after surface changes;
- GameApp suppression of ordinary mining while a surface mode owns the Pickaxe;
- unified Action-button priority;
- normal Pickaxe swing/durability usage.

## Device verification

After deployment, verify on Android/PWA:

- equip Pickaxe and confirm the compact terrain dock appears top-right;
- expand it and switch among Raise, Lower, Dig, Smoothen and Level;
- in Raise/Lower/Smoothen/Level, confirm the circular brush follows reachable ground under the white dot and the correct Action caption is shown;
- repeatedly Raise and Lower an open patch and confirm Ranger collision follows the changed surface;
- create a rough patch, Smoothen it, then Level it and confirm the result is visibly progressive rather than a hard vertical step;
- switch to Dig and confirm ordinary rock mining still works;
- Dig into ordinary inland solid ground and confirm no ocean/water sheet appears under the terrain;
- continue horizontally/downward to create an underground room and confirm 3D walls, floor and ceiling remain mineable;
- terraform near an existing tunnel entrance and confirm its opening and underground shell remain coherent;
- confirm grass/ferns/ground-cover reproject to changed terrain rather than floating;
- save, Continue, and confirm both surface edits and underground excavation return together;
- inspect large trees/rocks around aggressively edited terrain for any presentation issue that should be handled by their owning systems in a later pass.
